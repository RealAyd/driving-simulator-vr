# G1 Spatial prototype: verification report

Branch `rebuild/3d-judgement-simulator`, run on 2026-10-16 in a cloud container. Browser: HeadlessChrome 141.0.7390.37, WebGL 2 via ANGLE on **SwiftShader (software rendering, no GPU)**, 1280×720, desktop quality profile.

## What G1 adds

- **Working interior mirror** (`src/cockpit/mirror.ts`): a second camera in the same world renders the view behind the car into the mirror glass every frame (desktop) or every second frame (phone). The learner car's own meshes are excluded, so the mirror sees out of the back of the car. The image is reversed left/right like a real mirror. Its projection is fixed to the mirror's own 3.5:1 shape with a 24° horizontal field of view.
- **Smaller download**: Babylon is imported per module via `src/engine/babylon.ts`, and WebXR loads only when "Enter VR" is pressed.
- **Frame-time probe** (`src/engine/perfProbe.ts`): average FPS plus median, 95th-percentile and worst frame times.
- Location blocks accept an optional `colour`. Two parked cars behind the start point (red on the left kerb, blue on the right) serve as mirror references.

## Performance

Measured over a 6 s drive (`docs/verification/G1/perf-headless-swiftshader.json`): 8.5 fps average, frame time median 117 ms, 95th percentile 194 ms, worst 220 ms.

**This is software rendering on a CPU and is not representative of any real device.** It must not be compared with the 60 fps desktop, 30 fps phone or headset refresh-rate targets. No real-device measurement exists yet; that needs a real browser on a named desktop, phone and headset (see "Next measurements").

## Issues found and fixed during G1

1. Deep imports dropped Babylon's ray support, which would have broken `camera.getForwardRay()` at runtime. Caught by a unit test; fixed with an explicit side-effect import.
2. The first mirror test counted red pixels by area. An interior mirror shows a thin strip, so a more distant car shows more of its height while getting narrower, and the area barely changed. The test now measures width.
3. The mirror camera's projection took its shape from the canvas (16:9) rather than the mirror (3.5:1), which narrowed the view to about 12° and stretched it. The projection is now fixed explicitly.

## Known limitations

- One mirror only (interior). Door mirrors are G3.
- The mirror image is taken from the mirror's position looking straight back, not a full reflection of the driver's eye position, so moving the head does not change what the mirror shows. Acceptable for observation training at this stage; revisit with the cockpit model in G3.
- The mirror glass looks slanted on screen because it sits up and to the left of the driver with a wide (65° vertical) driver field of view.
- Placeholder geometry throughout; no other road users yet (G2), so the mirror only shows static objects.
