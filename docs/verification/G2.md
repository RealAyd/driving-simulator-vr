# G2 Complete exercise: verification report

Branch `rebuild/3d-judgement-simulator`, 2026-10-17. Same environment as G1 (HeadlessChrome 141 on SwiftShader software rendering, 1280×720).

## The exercise

**Zebra crossing beyond a parked van** (`content/scenarios/crossing-restricted-view.json`) on the representative residential street.

- A van is parked on the learner's side 5 m before a zebra crossing, hiding the pavement where a pedestrian waits. The learner has to pass the van in the other lane.
- When the learner reaches the trigger zone (distance 120 m along the road), the pedestrian prepares for 1.5 s, then crosses at 1.3 m/s. The trigger fires once per attempt; sitting in the zone does not re-fire it.
- A grey car follows the learner's own path, keeping a gap with the Intelligent Driver Model, and stops behind it.
- The attempt ends on reaching the end zone (completed), any collision, leaving the route for 1.5 s (off any road surface or facing the wrong way), or the 240 s time limit.
- If the learner stops early, the pedestrian keeps waiting and the HUD says "Continue along the road towards the crossing" after 8 s. There is no reverse gear (G0 decision), so "reversing" cannot occur. Pause freezes everything; restart and "Try again" reset all modules.

## Assessment (draft rubric `crossing-restricted-view` 0.1-draft, unreviewed)

| Rule | Evidence | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| `approach.speed` | Max speed in the restricted-view zone (zone entry and exit events) | over 25 mph serious; 20–25 mph minor; otherwise a positive note |
| `crossing.give-way` | Car on the striped area while the pedestrian is crossing (pedestrian state event) | serious |
| `crossing.too-close` | Car within 1.5 m of the pedestrian while moving | dangerous (same incident as give-way, upgrades it) |
| `crossing.collision` | Collision event with the pedestrian | dangerous (same incident) |
| `crossing.stop-position` | Stopped past the give-way line while the pedestrian is preparing or crossing | minor |
| `observation.mirror-before-braking` | No interior-mirror glance in the 5 s before the first braking in the approach | minor if a car is within 40 m behind, otherwise advice |
| `kerb.mounted` | Surface change to pavement | minor, one incident per mount |
| `vehicle.collision` | Collision with anything else | serious |

Each finding has a rule id, incident id, severity, supporting event ids, explanation and rubric version. The same incident is counted once; a later, more severe rule upgrades it and records `upgradedFrom`. Mirror glances are labelled by source ("mouse-controlled view" on desktop, "head direction" in VR); eye tracking is not claimed. Thresholds are training heuristics, **not DVSA criteria, and need instructor review** before the feedback is relied on. There is no "three repeats become serious" rule in this profile.

Attempts are saved on the device in IndexedDB (memory fallback where unavailable, and the results screen says so).

## Issues found and fixed during G2

1. **Startup crash**: the following car read the learner car's pose before the learner car existed ("Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'pose')"). The on-screen error screen reported it; construction order fixed.
2. **Stuck after hitting a parked vehicle**: with no reverse gear, a car pressed against the van could never continue. Any collision now ends the attempt.
3. **Lost final evidence**: when a collision ended the attempt, results were built before the assessor processed that step's events, so the collision finding was missing. The assessor now processes outstanding evidence before producing results.
4. Results headline said "Exercise complete" even when findings were dangerous; now "Exercise finished".

## Known limitations

- Placeholder visuals (box houses and van, cylinder pedestrian, primitive cars).
- Only the interior mirror; door mirrors and over-the-shoulder blind-spot checks come in G3.
- The pedestrian behaviour is scripted: it never hesitates or turns back, and only pauses if a car is physically in its path.
- The following car follows the learner's driven path rather than lanes, so it is not yet a general traffic model (lane following, stop lines and junction priorities remain for later scenarios).
- The "too close" explanation records the distance when first detected, not the minimum.
- Zig-zag markings around the crossing are not drawn.
- No instructor has reviewed the exercise or rubric.
- Performance is still unmeasured on real devices; the software renderer here runs about 8 fps with all G2 actors.
